    Number 1. Policy & Regulation Hacker News

    Unsealed Briefs in Authors’ Case v. Microsoft/OpenAI

    Newly unsealed briefs in Authors Guild v. OpenAI/Microsoft reveal internal communications showing executives and employees allegedly knew they were training on copyrighted books sourced from a 'sketchy Russian website' and understood their products would cause job losses. The filings add to a growing evidentiary record in the ongoing authors' copyright suit against both companies.

    Why it matters This is among the most consequential AI copyright cases in the US, and internal admissions of knowingly using pirated training data could weaken the industry's fair-use defenses across dozens of similar suits. A finding against OpenAI/Microsoft could force retraining, licensing renegotiations, or damages that reshape how all major labs source training data going forward.

    What to watch Watch for OpenAI and Microsoft's formal response to the unsealed evidence and whether the judge allows the 'knowledge of illegality' claims to proceed toward trial or summary judgment.

    Number 2. Infrastructure & Chips enterpriseai.economictimes.indiatimes.com

    Google tests putting AI chips in space through the Suncatcher satellite mission

    Google will send prototype satellites carrying its TPU hardware aboard SpaceX's Transporter-18 mission as part of its 'Suncatcher' program, testing radiation tolerance, thermal performance, and high-bandwidth laser links for potential orbital AI compute infrastructure.

    Why it matters As terrestrial power and land constraints increasingly bottleneck AI data center expansion, space-based compute is moving from theoretical to tested reality among hyperscalers. Success here could open a new front in the AI infrastructure race, with solar power and thermal dissipation advantages that ground-based facilities can't match.

    What to watch Watch for mission telemetry results on radiation-hardened TPU performance and whether Google or rivals like Microsoft and Amazon announce follow-on orbital compute programs.

    Number 3. Policy & Regulation tbsnews.net

    AI leaders warn the United Nations about security risks from increasingly powerful systems

    Executives from OpenAI, Anthropic and Hugging Face briefed the UN Security Council on AI risks, with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei warning that poorly managed AI could dangerously 'aggregate power.' The session, prompted by UN Secretary-General Guterres's warnings about AI slipping beyond human control, comes as the US and China maintain sharply different oversight approaches.

    Why it matters This marks AI safety concerns reaching the UN's highest security body, an unusual venue underscoring how mainstream existential-risk framing has become even as the Trump administration publicly favors minimal regulation. The divergence between US, Chinese and international-body approaches will shape whether any binding global AI governance framework emerges.

    What to watch Watch for any formal Security Council statement or resolution on AI risk, and how the Trump administration responds to pressure for international coordination it has resisted domestically.

    Anthropic considers releasing a new model ahead of a potential IPO

    Anthropic is reportedly considering releasing a new AI model to counter momentum from OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra ahead of an expected IPO, according to three sources. Enterprise expense platform Ramp data shows Astra now capturing about 13% of tracked enterprise AI spending versus roughly 8% for Anthropic's Claude Fable.

    Why it matters The move highlights tension between Amodei's public calls for an industry-wide slowdown and Anthropic's commercial need to defend market share ahead of a public listing. The Ramp figures offer a rare quantified snapshot of enterprise AI competition, showing OpenAI extending its lead in paid usage.

    What to watch Watch for confirmation of a new Anthropic model launch and details on IPO timing or filing as the company balances safety messaging against competitive necessity.
