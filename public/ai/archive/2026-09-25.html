    Number 1. Security & Safety Hacker News

    Goodbye Google

    A Google employee published a public resignation letter announcing their departure, stating their team's mission to make AI cheaper and lower-latency conflicts with their belief that AI progress is currently too rapid. The employee said they could not find another team at Google that wouldn't also accelerate AI development, and expressed broader doubts about where AI is heading.

    Why it matters Public, principled resignations from inside major AI labs are a visible form of internal dissent that surfaces safety concerns big labs would rather keep quiet. As scaling races intensify across Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic, employee pushback like this adds pressure on companies to justify pace-of-development decisions and could embolden similar exits or whistleblowing elsewhere in the industry.

    What to watch Watch for whether other researchers at Google or peer labs follow with public statements, and how Google responds internally or publicly to the departure.

    Number 2. Policy & Regulation abcnews.com

    Attorneys general from 23 states, Washington, D.C., and American Samoa demand comprehensive federal AI regulation

    Attorneys general from 23 states, Washington D.C., and American Samoa sent a letter to congressional leaders demanding comprehensive federal regulation and safety protocols for AI. The letter warns that unchecked AI development endangers Americans and threatens national security and critical infrastructure.

    Why it matters A coordinated, bipartisan multistate demand for federal action signals mounting political pressure on Congress to move beyond the current patchwork of state AI laws. It also reflects state officials' frustration with regulatory gaps exposed by recent incidents, and raises compliance stakes for AI companies operating across jurisdictions.

    What to watch Watch for congressional response or draft legislation, and whether additional states or federal agencies join the push for unified AI rules.
