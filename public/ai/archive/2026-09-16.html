    Number 3. Security & Safety The Hacker News

    Cisco Secure Email Gateway Flaw Exploited in the Wild, Enables Root Command Execution

    Cisco disclosed CVE-2026-76461, a critical vulnerability (CVSS 9.8) in AsyncOS Software for Cisco Secure Email Gateway that is actively being exploited in the wild. The flaw involves insufficient validation in email parsing logic, allowing unauthenticated remote attackers to execute arbitrary commands with root privileges via crafted email messages containing malicious SQL statements.

    Why it matters This represents an active, critical threat to email infrastructure at scale. Cisco Secure Email Gateway is widely deployed across enterprises, and a root-level remote code execution vulnerability being actively exploited creates immediate risk for data exfiltration, lateral movement, and system compromise. The attack vector (email) is ubiquitous and difficult to defend against comprehensively.

    What to watch Monitor for patch adoption rates and any reports of successful exploitation campaigns. Watch for related vulnerabilities in other email gateway products and whether this triggers broader security audits of email infrastructure.

    Number 4. Security & Safety Hacker News

    America's Driver's License Breach Is a National Security Disaster

    A dark web service called Nexus has been selling access to 153 million U.S. and Canadian driver's licenses and 3 million travel documents, representing a massive breach of identity documents. The breach is assessed to pose significant national security risks beyond typical cybercrime, as the data will likely be leveraged by foreign intelligence services.

    Why it matters This breach represents one of the largest compromises of government-issued identity documents in recent history. The scale and nature of the data make it uniquely valuable for both criminal identity theft and state-level intelligence operations. The breach undermines the security model of driver's licenses as identity verification and creates cascading risks across financial services, travel, and government systems that rely on these documents.

    What to watch Monitor for downstream impacts including identity theft waves, fraudulent travel document use, and any public attribution of the breach source. Watch for policy responses around identity verification standards and whether this accelerates adoption of digital identity systems.

    We got admin access to Baseten's production GitHub in 25 minutes

    Security researchers using an autonomous hacking agent called Strix gained admin-level GitHub access to Baseten's production repositories in 25 minutes. The researchers discovered a live GitHub token with repository-level admin rights on internal Baseten repos, raising questions about the security posture of a $13 billion-valued company handling customer data and AI models.

    Why it matters This incident demonstrates that even well-funded, security-conscious AI infrastructure companies can have critical vulnerabilities in their access control and secrets management. The speed of compromise (25 minutes) and the level of access gained (production admin) suggest fundamental security gaps. This is particularly concerning given Baseten's role as a trusted inference provider for serious companies, and it validates the emerging threat model of autonomous hacking agents.

    What to watch Monitor whether Baseten discloses the root cause and remediation steps publicly. Watch for similar findings against other AI infrastructure providers and whether this triggers broader security audits of the inference/model hosting ecosystem.
