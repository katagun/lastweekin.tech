Today's AI news centers on mounting safety anxiety inside major labs, OpenAI's shifting AGI timeline rhetoric, a new Microsoft routing/verification model, and a UK policy move aimed at loosening talent restrictions for AI startups.

  1. 1

    Number 1. Security & Safety online91.thedailystar.net

    Researchers and former AI-lab employees call for a pause in the race toward self-improving systems

    Current and former staff at OpenAI and Google DeepMind gave video testimonials to AI safety nonprofit Palisade Research, warning that their employers are doing too little to guard against the dangers of self-improving AI systems that could slip beyond human control. The project, called frominside.ai, was designed to let insiders speak directly to the public rather than through social media. Some participants said their concerns are genuine rather than a marketing posture.

    Why it matters This is a notable break from the usual pattern of safety concerns being aired through op-eds or anonymous leaks: insiders at two of the most influential labs are going on record, via video, to warn about self-improving systems specifically. Coming amid intensifying competitive pressure between OpenAI, Google DeepMind and others to ship more capable agentic models, the testimonials add weight to calls for slower, more cautious development and could influence public and regulatory sentiment at a moment when trust in AI labs is already fragile.

    What to watch Watch whether more employees go on record through frominside.ai, and whether OpenAI or DeepMind leadership issues any public response to the specific self-improvement concerns raised.

  2. 2

    Number 2. Companies ground.news

    OpenAI is reported to be targeting an internal AGI system by the end of 2026

    Sam Altman told Time that "we've all been too ambitious on timelines" and that "people are pretty negative on AI" and hate data centers right now, even as OpenAI keeps testing its Astra project and pursues an internal AGI target. Governors in Pennsylvania and Texas are reportedly seeking to curtail future data-center development after previously promoting it. Altman attributed slow AI adoption to weak public trust and businesses' resistance to changing established habits.

    Why it matters This marks a rare moment of public timeline-walkback from OpenAI's CEO, acknowledging a gap between the industry's stated ambitions and both public sentiment and real-world adoption speed. The admission comes as political backlash against data-center buildout grows at the state level, suggesting infrastructure and social-license friction, not just technical limits, could become a binding constraint on the AGI race. It also signals OpenAI may be recalibrating external messaging even while internal AGI targets persist.

    What to watch Watch for how OpenAI frames its internal AGI timeline going forward, and whether more state governments move to restrict data-center expansion.

    Number 3. Capabilities aiweekly.co

    Microsoft releases Decision-1, a classifier for routing, verification, and workflow control

    Microsoft released Decision-1, a classifier model designed for routing, verification, and workflow control tasks. The release positions the model as infrastructure for orchestrating decisions within larger AI agent pipelines rather than as a general-purpose chat model.

    Why it matters As AI deployments shift from single-model chat interfaces to multi-step agentic workflows, specialized routing and verification components become critical infrastructure — determining which model or tool handles a given task and catching errors before they propagate. Microsoft entering this niche signals growing enterprise demand for reliability layers around frontier models, and could pressure competitors to ship similar control infrastructure rather than just bigger foundation models.

    What to watch Watch for benchmarks or enterprise case studies showing how Decision-1 performs against rival routing/verification tools from OpenAI, Anthropic, or open-source alternatives.

    Number 4. Policy & Regulation aiweekly.co

    UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham pledges legislation to remove non-compete clauses for startups and scaling firms

    UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham pledged legislation to remove non-compete clauses for startups and scaling firms, a move aimed at easing talent mobility in fast-growing sectors including AI.

    Why it matters Non-compete restrictions have been cited as a drag on startup ecosystems' ability to attract and retain AI talent, particularly as competition for researchers and engineers intensifies globally. A UK policy shift to loosen these restrictions could make Britain more competitive for AI talent relative to the US and EU, where enforceability varies by jurisdiction, potentially accelerating startup formation and researcher mobility within the UK's AI sector.

    What to watch Watch for the specific legislative text and timeline, and whether other governments follow with similar talent-mobility reforms aimed at AI competitiveness.
