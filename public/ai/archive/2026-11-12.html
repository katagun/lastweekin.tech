The 5 AI stories that mattered Briefing for Nov 12, 2026

An archived briefing. See today’s briefing.

The day's biggest AI story is Nvidia's leadership crisis boiling over into Jensen Huang's resignation, while Anthropic cements its position as the first frontier AI lab to go public and Google pushes capabilities forward with a new decision-scoring API and a genomics model.

  1. 1

    Number 1. Companies The Verge

    Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has resigned

    Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned effective immediately after The New York Times reported a second alleged extramarital affair, this time with VP of healthcare Kimberly Powell, who reports directly to him. This followed an earlier acknowledged affair with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, who US intelligence reportedly identified as an asset linked to China's Ministry of State Security. CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO, and former chip exec Harvey Jones was appointed Nvidia's first board chairman.

    Why it matters Nvidia sits at the center of the global AI compute buildout, and the abrupt departure of its founder-CEO during an active national-security-adjacent scandal creates real uncertainty for an industry whose capital flows, chip roadmaps, and export-control posture have all been shaped around Huang's leadership. Shares fell more than 11% on the news, and commentators are already flagging risk to the circular AI financing arrangements Huang helped orchestrate across hyperscalers and chipmakers.

    What to watch Watch for Nvidia's board process on selecting a permanent CEO, any fallout in AI infrastructure financing deals tied to Huang's relationships, and further disclosures about the alleged China intelligence connection.

  2. 2

    Number 2. Industry & Economics Hacker News

    Anthropic IPO

    Anthropic published its IPO prospectus, disclosing $17.0 billion in trailing revenue, more than 12x year-over-year growth, and over 11,900 customers with trailing-twelve-month revenue of at least $100,000. The filing frames Anthropic as a public benefit corporation aiming to secure AI's benefits while mitigating its risks, and includes a roadshow video ahead of listing.

    Why it matters This makes Anthropic the first frontier AI lab to pursue public markets, a milestone that will force unprecedented financial transparency on model costs, compute spend, and safety commitments for a company previously funded entirely through private rounds. The IPO will set a valuation benchmark for the entire frontier AI sector and test whether public investors buy into AI lab economics amid growing scrutiny of circular financing deals.

    What to watch Watch for the IPO pricing and opening-day valuation, and how rivals like OpenAI respond to having a public comparable in the market.

  3. 3

    Number 3. Capabilities Hacker News

    Gemini Decisions API

    Google launched the Gemini Decisions API, powered by a new model called Gemini Decision 1, designed for fast closed-set choices like tool selection, policy checks, model routing, and context grading rather than open-ended text generation. It accepts multimodal input across text, images, audio, video, and PDFs up to 256K tokens, and can answer up to 1,000 independent questions in a single call.

    Why it matters This signals a shift toward purpose-built, cheaper inference primitives for the narrow decision-making tasks that increasingly sit inside agentic pipelines, rather than relying on full generative models for every step. Positioning it as a distinct API/model category suggests Google is trying to own the infrastructure layer for agent orchestration, not just the flagship chat model market.

    What to watch Watch adoption by agent-framework builders and whether competitors like OpenAI or Anthropic respond with similar bounded-decision endpoints.

  4. 4

    Number 4. Capabilities thefile.news

    Google DeepMind releases AlphaGenome 2

    Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2, a model that predicts how a genetic variant changes gene activity within a specific human cell type. The system addresses the challenge that all cells share identical DNA but activate different genes depending on cell type, such as heart muscle versus immune cells.

    Why it matters Cell-type-specific variant-to-expression prediction is a core bottleneck in interpreting genomic data for disease research and drug discovery; improving this could accelerate identification of disease-causing mutations and target genes. It extends DeepMind's track record of applying frontier AI to biology beyond protein structure into functional genomics, reinforcing Google's strategy of using capability gains for scientific acceleration claims.

    What to watch Watch for independent validation of AlphaGenome 2's predictions and whether pharmaceutical or research partners adopt it for variant interpretation pipelines.

  5. 5

    Number 5. Policy & Regulation Hacker News

    United Airlines bans AI agents from booking flights on behalf of travelers

    United Airlines announced it is banning unauthorized third-party AI agents from booking flights, checking passengers in, or making account changes on travelers' behalf. The airline cited the risk of sharing account credentials and payment information with personal AI assistants, warning that resulting actions could have unintended consequences.

    Why it matters This is one of the first explicit corporate policy responses to the rise of autonomous AI agents acting as consumer proxies, and it highlights a real friction point: agentic AI needs delegated credentials to be useful, but companies see that as a security and liability risk. Expect other regulated consumer-facing industries (banking, healthcare, travel) to follow with similar restrictions as agent-based commerce tools proliferate.

    What to watch Watch whether other airlines or travel platforms adopt similar bans, and whether AI agent companies respond with standardized, auditable delegation protocols.
