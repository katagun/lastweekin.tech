    Anthropic's 'watermark' text adulteration in Claude is a perversion of writing

    # Summary Anthropic announced that all Claude models worldwide will watermark their generated text to comply with EU regulations, but initially provided no explanation of how the system would work. The watermarking uses steganography—subtly biasing word choices toward a "green list" and away from a "red list" during text generation, creating statistical fingerprints detectable only by Anthropic with a secret key, though this approach necessarily compromises the quality and precision of the generated text. Critic John Gruber argues that this implementation adulterates Claude's output even in private conversations, makes the writing worse for users without providing them any benefit, and will be easily circumvented by bad actors while unfairly flagging legitimate uses of AI assistance.

    Number 2. Open Source Hacker News

    Firefox is now the last major browser that still supports uBlock Origin

    Firefox announced it will continue supporting uBlock Origin, positioning itself as the only major browser to maintain full compatibility with the ad-blocking extension as Microsoft Edge and other Chromium-based browsers phase it out under Manifest V3. Microsoft Edge, Opera, Brave, Vivaldi, and Samsung Browser are all following Google's lead in migrating from Manifest V2 to V3, which will prevent ad-blocking extensions from accessing the functions needed to identify and block ads. Safari and DuckDuckGo, the other major non-Chromium browsers, do not support uBlock Origin, leaving Firefox as the sole option for users wanting the full-featured extension without compromises. Users on other browsers will be limited to uBlock Origin Lite, which has reduced functionality, or their browser's built-in ad-blocking features.

    Number 5. AI Hacker News

    Why does Opus 5 feel worse to work with?

    # Summary The author argues that Anthropic's Opus 5 model feels worse to work with than earlier versions like Opus 4.7, Opus 4.8, and Fable, despite having superior benchmark performance and capabilities. While Opus 5 is more capable overall, the author and colleagues report that it requires more "careful babysitting" because it makes assumptions without checking, doesn't ask clarifying questions when intent is unclear, and reinterprets plans without asking—behaviors that differ from the earlier models' tendency to seek clarification. The author speculates this results from Anthropic's focus on benchmark performance and self-improving AI development, which inherently selects for models that make bold assumptions under ambiguity rather than pause to ask questions, an approach that works well for self-contained benchmark tasks but poorly for real-world coding work where ambiguity and unclear context are inevitable.

    Why it made the cut: 970 points on Hacker News

    Number 6. AI Hacker News

    Gemini 3.7 Flash

    Google has released Gemini 3.7 Flash, an upgraded version of its Flash language model designed for coding, web development, and agent workflows, arriving just three weeks after Gemini 3.6 Flash. The new model shows significant performance improvements across software engineering tasks, achieving 43.6% accuracy on FrontierCode 1.1 Main compared to 3.6 Flash's 34.4%, and is priced at half the cost of its predecessor at $0.75 per million input tokens through the end of the year. Gemini 3.7 Flash is available to developers through Google's Gemini API and Android Studio, to enterprises through the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, and to individual users via Gemini Spark for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. The model includes updated safety safeguards against misuse in chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear, and cyber offense domains.

    France's top court blocks social media ban for under-15s

    France's Constitutional Council struck down a law this week that would have banned children under 15 from accessing social media platforms like Facebook and X, ruling that the legislation infringed on freedom of expression and communication. The bill had passed through France's National Assembly and Senate with broad bipartisan support and would have also prohibited smartphone use in schools while exempting educational resources like Wikipedia and GitHub. President Emmanuel Macron, who championed the ban as protection against American tech companies and Chinese algorithms, now plans to revise and reintroduce the legislation before spring. Other European countries including Norway, Britain, and Greece are pursuing similar social media bans for minors, though Australia's recent attempt showed limited effectiveness, with studies indicating approximately 85% of banned under-16 users still accessing their accounts through various methods.
