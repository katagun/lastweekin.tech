    Building Pi with Pi

    The article reflects on using Pi, an AI‑powered issue tracker and development tool, to build and debug Pi itself, highlighting the “slop” that arises when LLMs rewrite issue descriptions and code, producing vague, over‑hypothesised, or incorrect diagnostics that mislead human maintainers; this sloppiness forces developers to insist on concise, fact‑based issue reports and to actively verify AI analysis rather than trust it, and it also leads to over‑engineering fixes that add unnecessary complexity. The author notes the high volume of AI‑generated issues and pull requests that are auto‑closed, reopened, or closed again, stressing that the problem lies more with contributors and the tools they use than with GitHub itself. Parallel Pi sessions can work on multiple issues simultaneously, but the overall ecosystem remains overwhelmed by the sheer amount of code and projects in a post‑AI world, underscoring the need for stronger community collaboration and a focus on fixing upstream bugs rather than creating local workarounds.

    Number 4. AI WIRED

    The AI Era Is Creating a Bug Hunting Arms Race

    A decade of bug‑bounty programs—once a reluctant novelty—has been upended by the rise of agentic AI that can autonomously hunt software vulnerabilities and craft exploits, flooding disclosure portals and inflating the cost of payouts. Apple’s rewards have leapt from $200k to $2m, and firms like Google expect to spend many times more next year, while smaller companies may struggle to keep pace. The surge in low‑ and medium‑severity findings is already shortening the 90‑day responsible‑disclosure window that was built for slower, rarer bug‑finders, and is prompting organizations to speed patch releases to avoid being outmaneuvered by attackers who are now using the same AI tools. This new equilibrium threatens to compress discovery and exploitation timelines, making rapid, careful patch deployment—despite its operational risks—an urgent imperative in a landscape where even “non‑professional” actors can deploy AI‑generated zero‑day exploits.
