    Number 2. General Tech BleepingComputer

    Critical Everest Forms Pro flaw exploited to take over WordPress sites

    Hackers are actively exploiting CVE‑2026‑3300, a critical vulnerability in Everest Forms Pro (v 1.9.12 and earlier) that lets an unauthenticated attacker inject arbitrary PHP code via the plugin’s Complex Calculation feature. The flaw arises because user‑supplied values are inserted into a PHP string and executed with `eval()`, and the sanitization used (`sanitize_text_field()`) fails to escape single quotes, enabling attackers to close the string and inject code such as `wp_insert_user()` to create rogue administrator accounts. Wordfence reports show active exploitation began on April 13, with over 29,300 blocked attempts and two main source IPs (202.56.2.126 and 209.146.60.26). The issue was reported in February, patched on March 18, but administrators should still audit logs and accounts—especially for the username “diksimarina”—and block the identified IPs to mitigate the risk.

    Number 3. AI Ars Technica

    The weather and climate science AI revolution isn’t revolutionary

    The article explains that AI is increasingly being used in weather and climate science, but not as flashy large‑language models (LLMs) like chatbots; instead it relies on established machine learning (ML) techniques that identify complex patterns in vast datasets. While a National Weather Service office once posted a misleading AI‑generated map, the real applications involve training neural networks to recognise relationships in environmental data, improving forecasting and climate modeling. The piece clarifies that ML, not LLMs, underpins these advances and that meteorologists and climate scientists are still doing the heavy lifting, using well‑understood algorithms rather than replacing them with generic AI assistants.

    Number 4. AI WIRED

    Momfluencers Are Pitching AI as a Better ‘Coparent’ Than Men

    Lilian Schmidt, a Zurich‑based brand consultant and mother, turned to ChatGPT after conventional sleep‑tips failed to help her 3½‑year‑old daughter fall asleep, discovering that the AI’s counterintuitive advice—more stimulation before bed—worked instantly; this success sparked a viral TikTok series and the creation of a paid custom GPT called “Coparent,” positioning Schmidt as a “momfluencer” who promotes generative AI as a substitute for traditional parenting labor. Her content, which highlights how AI can reduce the invisible workload that 13.5 extra hours per week of chores and childcare places on U.S. mothers, mirrors broader gendered labor disparities, with women still shouldering the majority of household responsibilities despite fathers’ increasing involvement. Yet a 2025 study shows women are 20 % less likely than men to use generative AI, a gap attributed to the tools’ “pale, male, and stale” design, underscoring both the potential and the challenges of AI‑assisted parenting.

    Number 7. General Tech WIRED

    Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5x Review: The Best Laptop Under $1,000

    Lenovo’s IdeaPad Slim 5x proves that a budget laptop can feel premium without compromising too much on usability. Its 15.3‑inch screen, centered touchpad and spacious keyboard keep it travel‑ready while matching the size and thickness of the 15‑inch MacBook Air. The touchpad outperforms many rivals in responsiveness, though its click is noticeably loud, and the speakers, while not as full as Apple’s, outperform average budget units. Powered by the Qualcomm Snapdragon X2 Plus, the machine delivers solid CPU performance for multitasking, though it still lags behind Apple’s M5 in raw power. Still, for typical office workflows, streaming, and light multitasking the X2 Plus offers more than enough horsepower. The bright, colorful, anti‑glare touchscreen adds a premium feel, making the Slim 5x a compelling choice for users who value portability, performance, and a decent display without a hefty price tag.
